🔍 Why Was Your Laptop Lagging & Getting Hot?

1. External Monitor Connection (HDMI hardwired to RTX 3060): Your external monitor connects via HDMI, which on Lenovo Legion 5 laptops is hardwired directly to the discrete NVIDIA RTX 3060 graphics card. This keeps the NVIDIA card continuously active at ~14.5W idle. Because the CPU and GPU share copper heatpipes, this baseline heat keeps the chassis warm (50°C–52°C) even when no 3D software is open.
2. Intel Aggressive Boost Voltage Spikes: Windows defaulted the i7-11800H processor to "Aggressive" boost, causing CPU cores to jump to 4.60 GHz (60W–80W power draw) on minor background apps. When that 80W met the GPU's 14.5W, thermal limits were briefly reached, causing Windows to throttle CPU clocks and produce micro-stutters.
3. Orphaned Ghost Processes & Startup Bloat: Headless RobloxPlayerBeta and RobloxCrashHandler had been running in the background without any window for ~4 hours, churning over 1,900 CPU seconds. Roblox had also placed itself in auto-start on every boot.
4. Unbounded WSL2 Virtual Machine RAM: WSL2 was dynamically assigned up to 32 GB of memory without a ceiling, competing with Windows memory caching.

💡 Quick Lenovo Legion Tips

  • Thermal Profiles (Fn + Q): Look at the power button LED color. White is Balanced (best for daily schoolwork), Blue is Quiet (low power cap, absolute silence), and Red is Performance (full 130W GPU wattage for heavy rendering and gaming).
  • External Monitor: The rear/side vents will always feel warm when an external screen is plugged into HDMI because the RTX 3060 is actively powering the display. This is completely normal hardware operation.
